Privacy

No accounts, cookies, ads or analytics, no tracking, and nothing sold. Here's exactly what Pallot sends and keeps.

Last updated October 7, 2026

In short

  • Your picks, notes and pick rule never leave your browser. We never see how you plan to vote.
  • We don't track you, build a profile of you, or sell anything.
  • To find your ballot, your address goes to the US Census (and to a backup only if it can't match it), and what you type goes to Ballotpedia for suggestions.
  • Our server keeps a copy of each lookup, with nothing that says who asked, so it doesn't ask twice.

In your browser

  • Your picks, notes, write-ins, collapsed races and pick rule. Never sent to our server.
  • Your last lookup: the address, election, ballot and any numbers you entered.
  • Which sources you turned on or off. Sent with each request so the server builds your ballot with them, but never saved there.
  • Your appearance, search and view choices.
  • Your ban list. Never sent to our server.

All of it stays until you clear it in Settings → Clear data. Private windows forget it when they close.

Each is asked only while it's on in Settings. Everything else is the same for everyone and is downloaded whole: precinct maps, polls, key dates, seat holders, and endorsement lists. TrackAIPAC, Vote for Peace and Texas Ethics Commission data come with Pallot.

When we refresh saved copies, saved addresses, district numbers and locations are sent again, but never typed text or map tiles.

Straight from your browser

  • Candidate photos load from Ballotpedia, which sees your internet address.
  • Links you open go straight to that site. A web search sends the candidate's name, office, place and year, and an Issues link the issue too; an AI assistant may keep it in your chat history.

Everything else, including the map, comes through our server.
