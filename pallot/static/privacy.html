Privacy

Pallot has no accounts, cookies, ads or analytics. This page lists what it sends, what it keeps, where, and for how long.

Last updated October 3, 2026

In short

  • Your picks, notes, write-ins and pick rule stay in your browser. They're never sent anywhere.
  • To find your ballot, Pallot sends your address to the US Census geocoder, and its location to Ballotpedia if Ballotpedia is on.
  • While you type your address, Pallot sends what you've typed so far to Ballotpedia's address search for suggestions, unless you turn them off in Settings.
  • For the map of your districts, Pallot sends the US Census your district numbers, never your address, and asks OpenStreetMap for the street map of the area you look at, unless you turn the street map off in Settings.
  • The Pallot server keeps a copy of those lookups and suggestions, so the same text doesn't have to be sent again, until they're cleared in Settings. Suggestions, and addresses that weren't found, are deleted by themselves the first time Pallot starts after they're two months old (one month, if nothing was found).

Who holds your data

Pallot is a program that runs on a computer, which this page calls the Pallot server. If you started Pallot on your own computer, as it's meant to be used, the server is your computer: what it keeps is visible only to people who use that computer. If someone else runs Pallot for you, the server is theirs, and they can see what it keeps.

When you look up a ballot

Your browser sends the Pallot server your address, the election, the primary ballot you chose and any district or precinct numbers you entered, and, while you type the address, what you've typed so far. The server then asks these services for you. They see the server's internet address rather than your browser's, which is the same thing if Pallot runs on your computer.

Refresh in Settings sends the saved requests again: refreshing "Address lookup & districts" re-sends every address in the server's copy, refreshing "Address suggestions" re-sends everything saved from the address box, refreshing "District map" re-sends every saved district number (the street map can't be refreshed: OpenStreetMap asks that its tiles only be fetched as they're viewed), and refreshing Ballotpedia re-sends every saved location.

Six sources never receive anything about you. The Texas Legislative Council's maps are downloaded whole, and the server finds your State Board of Education district and your election precinct in them and draws their outlines from them: its district map once, and its precinct map on the first lookup and again only when the Council publishes a newer one (the server asks its portal at most once a week, sending nothing about you, if election precincts are on). TrackAIPAC's, Vote for Peace's and the Texas Ethics Commission's data come with Pallot, and are only fetched again from trackaipac.com, voteforpeace.info or the Texas Ethics Commission when you press Refresh in Settings. FiftyPlusOne's poll lists cover the whole country and are the same for everyone, so the server downloads them (at most once a day, if Polls is on) and picks out your races itself. The key election dates come from one page on the Texas Secretary of State's website, the same for everyone, which the server downloads at most once a day if the key dates are on. Nor do the organizations whose endorsement lists come with Pallot: each list is a copy made once, part of Pallot itself, and never fetched. The organizations whose lists Pallot fetches from their websites get only a request for the whole public list (for some, after one for the page that hands out the token the list asks for), the same for everyone, which the server sends at most once a week for each list that's on.

Each service decides what it keeps of the requests it receives, under its own privacy policy.

Straight from your browser

  • Candidate photos come from Ballotpedia's image server when Ballotpedia is on. Like any site you load an image from, it sees your internet address. Pallot tells your browser not to say which page the photo is on.
  • Links you open, such as a candidate's web search, a source's page or a campaign website, go straight from your browser to that site. A web search sends the search engine you chose the candidate's name, office, place and election year.
  • Everything else, including the pages, scripts and icons, comes from the Pallot server. So do the calendar files of the key dates, which hold the election's dates and links, not your address. The street map's tiles come through the server too, so OpenStreetMap never sees your browser. Leaflet, the program that draws the map, comes with Pallot.

Apart from suggestions and addresses that weren't found, the times above only decide when Pallot asks a source again. They don't delete anything: an address that was found stays in the server's copy until it's cleared, as the copy to use when the Census doesn't answer.

To clear it, go to Settings. Clear on "Address lookup & districts" deletes every saved address lookup, Clear on "Address suggestions" deletes everything saved from the address box, Clear on Ballotpedia deletes its saved lookups, and "Clear all caches" deletes everything saved from every source. Clearing overwrites what it deletes, so it can't be read back from the files. You can also stop Pallot and delete its data folder (data/, unless PALLOT_DATA_DIR points elsewhere).

Addresses travel in the body of requests, which isn't logged. If something goes wrong, though, an error report in the log can include the request Pallot was making, address included.
