Skip to the ballot

Currently covers Texas addresses. Your address is sent to the US Census geocoder (and, if it can't match it, to OpenStreetMap and then Google, if this Pallot has a Google key; its location goes to Ballotpedia, if it's on) to find your districts, and is remembered in this browser. As you type, it's also sent to Ballotpedia for suggestions, unless they're off in Settings. More in Privacy.

    Your ballot, race by race

    Enter your home address to see every race you'll vote in.

    1. Compare sources. Each candidate shows how much money they've raised and from whom, what the state's filings, Ballotpedia, TrackAIPAC and Vote for Peace say, and where they stand in public polls, plus a web search. A race says when its seat is open, and which party holds it now.
    2. Pick and take notes. Choose a candidate in each race and jot down why. Collapse races as you go.
    3. Know the dates and your districts. The top of the ballot has the last day to register, early voting and Election Day, to add to your calendar, and your districts and election precinct, with a map of them. In seven of the largest counties, your commissioner and JP precincts too.
    4. Print your picks and take the sheet with you to vote.
