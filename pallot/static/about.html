About Pallot

A personal helper for working through your ballot: every race you'll vote in, what several sources say about each candidate, and a printout of your picks to take with you.

What it does

  • Enter your home address and Pallot shows every race on your ballot, with the candidates in ballot order.
  • Each candidate shows what the Texas Secretary of State's filings, Ballotpedia, TrackAIPAC and Vote for Peace say, and which source said what, with a link to search the web for more.
  • Congressional and state races show how much money each candidate has raised, from their reports to the FEC or the Texas Ethics Commission. Each candidate's details break it down: where the money came from, the largest donors and outside spending.
  • U.S. Senate, U.S. House and Governor races show where the candidates stand in public polls, when there are any.
  • The top of the ballot shows when to vote: the last day to register, early voting and Election Day, each of which you can add to your calendar. Beside it are your districts, as on your voter registration certificate, with a map of them.
  • Pick a candidate in each race, or let a rule of your own pick by party, TrackAIPAC, Vote for Peace, money and polls. Jot down why, and print your picks to take to the polls.

What it isn't

  • Not official. Pallot isn't affiliated with any government, election office, party or campaign, or with the sources it uses. Always check the official sample ballot from your county elections office.
  • Not a recommendation. Pallot shows what its sources say, side by side. It doesn't tell you how to vote.
  • Not where you register or vote. To check your registration or find your polling place, go to VoteTexas.gov; the ballot's "Am I registered?" button opens the state's My Voter Portal.

Candidates are matched across sources by name, with the seat and party to confirm, and every match is marked "Matched" or "Likely match". The FAQ explains more, including how the money figures are put together.

How it works

Pallot is a small program that runs on your own computer. It serves these pages and asks the sources on your behalf. It keeps a copy of every answer, so looking up the same address again doesn't ask anyone, even after a restart.

There are no accounts, ads or tracking. Your picks and notes stay in your browser; Privacy lists everything Pallot sends and keeps.

The name

The name joins Palestine and ballot, and the logo is the Palestinian flag. Pallot is made by Just-Peace, which stands for justice and peace.

Credits

Pallot is built on information from:

  • Texas Secretary of State: elections, ballot order and candidate filings, from its elections site, and the key dates of each election, from its Important Election Dates page. Official voting information is at VoteTexas.gov.
  • Ballotpedia: local races and others the state doesn't list, notes on races, precincts, and candidate profiles and photos, from ballotpedia.org, and address suggestions from its address search, with Esri's address data.
  • TrackAIPAC: lobby money and endorsements for congressional candidates, from trackaipac.com.
  • Vote for Peace: candidates rated ally, neutral or opposed by Organize for Peace, from voteforpeace.info, used with their permission.
  • Federal Election Commission: campaign finance for congressional candidates, from the OpenFEC API. More at fec.gov.
  • Texas Ethics Commission: campaign finance for state candidates and officeholders, from its campaign finance data.
  • FiftyPlusOne: public polls of U.S. Senate, U.S. House and Governor races, from fiftyplusone.news.
  • US Census Bureau: county and districts for your address, from the Census Geocoder, and the outlines of your districts, from TIGERweb.
  • Texas Legislative Council: the State Board of Education district map and the election precinct maps, from the Capitol Data Portal.
  • Counties: commissioner and JP precincts by election precinct, from the map servers of Harris, Dallas, Tarrant, Travis (its Tax Office), Fort Bend, Bexar and Denton counties.
  • OpenStreetMap: address search by Nominatim. The street map under your districts is OpenStreetMap's, from its tile server. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors.
  • Leaflet: the map of your districts is drawn with Leaflet, © Volodymyr Agafonkin and CloudMade, under the BSD 2-Clause license. It comes with Pallot, so it's never loaded from elsewhere.

Each candidate's "Web search" link goes to Google, or to the search engine you choose in Settings: Bing, DuckDuckGo, Brave Search, Yahoo, Startpage, Ecosia, Kagi or Perplexity.
