Privacy

VoteBot has no accounts, cookies, ads or analytics. This page lists what it sends, what it keeps, where, and for how long.

Last updated 28 September 2026

In short

  • Your picks, notes and write-ins stay in your browser. They're never sent anywhere.
  • To find your ballot, VoteBot sends your address to the US Census geocoder, and its location to Ballotpedia if Ballotpedia is on.
  • While you type your address, VoteBot sends what you've typed so far to Photon for suggestions, unless you turn them off in Settings.
  • The VoteBot server keeps a copy of those lookups and suggestions, so the same text doesn't have to be sent again, until they're cleared in Settings.

Who holds your data

VoteBot is a program that runs on a computer, which this page calls the VoteBot server. If you started VoteBot on your own computer, as it's meant to be used, the server is your computer: what it keeps is visible only to people who use that computer. If someone else runs VoteBot for you, the server is theirs, and they can see what it keeps.

When you look up a ballot

Your browser sends the VoteBot server your address, the election, the primary ballot you chose and any precinct numbers you entered, and, while you type the address, what you've typed so far. The server then asks these services for you. They see the server's internet address rather than your browser's, which is the same thing if VoteBot runs on your computer.

Refresh in Settings sends the saved requests again: refreshing "Address lookup & districts" re-sends every address in the server's copy, refreshing "Address suggestions" re-sends everything saved from the address box, and refreshing Ballotpedia re-sends every saved location.

Three sources never receive anything about you. The Texas Legislative Council's district map is downloaded once and searched on the server. TrackAIPAC's and the Texas Ethics Commission's data come with VoteBot, and are only fetched again from trackaipac.com or the Texas Ethics Commission when you press Refresh in Settings.

Each service decides what it keeps of the requests it receives, under its own privacy policy.

Straight from your browser

  • Candidate photos come from Ballotpedia's image server when Ballotpedia is on. Like any site you load an image from, it sees your internet address. VoteBot tells your browser not to say which page the photo is on.
  • Links you open, such as a candidate's web search, a source's page or a campaign website, go straight from your browser to that site. A web search sends the search engine you chose the candidate's name, office, place and election year.
  • Everything else, including the pages, scripts and icons, comes from the VoteBot server.

The times above only decide when VoteBot asks a source again. They don't delete anything: an address, or what you typed, stays in the server's copy until it's cleared.

To clear it, go to Settings. Clear on "Address lookup & districts" deletes every saved address lookup, Clear on "Address suggestions" deletes everything saved from the address box, Clear on Ballotpedia deletes its saved lookups, and "Clear all caches" deletes everything saved from every source. You can also stop VoteBot and delete its data folder (data/, unless VOTEBOT_DATA_DIR points elsewhere).

Addresses travel in the body of requests, which isn't logged. If something goes wrong, though, an error report in the log can include the request VoteBot was making, address included.

Changes to this page

If VoteBot starts sending or keeping something new, this page will say so, and the date at the top will change.
